Nalangu

Nalangu is a cherished Tamil Hindu wedding tradition performed before the wedding ceremony. It is a time of blessing, purification and preparation as the bride and groom begin their journey into married life.

Nalangu Traditions & Meaning

Traditionally, the bride and groom take part in separate Nalangu ceremonies, surrounded by their families and loved ones. During the ceremony, married women bless them with turmeric, sandalwood, kumkum, prayers and arthi, symbolising purity, prosperity, protection, good fortune and the removal of negativity and obstacles.

Three Nalangus are often observed: one for the bride, one for the groom, and a final Nalangu at the wedding venue before the marriage ceremony. The bride and groom traditionally do not see one another during this period, preserving the significance of their union on the wedding day.

Beyond its spiritual meaning, Nalangu is also a celebration of family and community. Through blessings, songs, laughter and shared traditions, loved ones come together to prepare the couple for a joyful and prosperous married life.

Nalangu represents

  • Purification and spiritual preparation Turmeric, sandalwood and kumkum cleanse and prepare the couple for marriage.
  • Blessings from elders and loved ones Prayers and arthi from married women carry the family’s blessings.
  • Prosperity, happiness and good fortune Wishes for abundance and for obstacles to be removed from the couple’s path.
  • Bringing two families together Both families gather, celebrate and become one.
  • A joyful start to the wedding celebrations Songs, laughter and shared traditions open the days ahead.
